VISIONDESIGNIE — SIMPLE EXPLANATIONS FOR USERS (RAG)

FSI — PLAIN WORDS
- FSI means: how much total floor area you may build compared to your plot.
- Think: plot is the base; FSI says how much “stacked floor” you can add in total.
- Higher FSI still needs setbacks and height rules — it is not a free pass to touch the boundary.

SETBACK — PLAIN WORDS
- Setback is empty space you leave inside your boundary, away from where the building sits.
- Front setback faces the road. Rear and sides have their own strips.
- Why it exists: light, air, privacy, fire access, and sometimes future road widening.

BUILDABLE FOOTPRINT — PLAIN WORDS
- After setbacks, the area left inside is where your walls can generally go on each floor.
- Stairs and lift eat area too — “usable rooms” are smaller than the footprint looks.

ZONING IN A HOME — PLAIN WORDS
- Public zone: where guests arrive — living, sometimes dining.
- Private zone: bedrooms and family areas.
- Service zone: kitchen, utility, toilets — short paths, easy maintenance.

CARPET VS BUILT-UP — PLAIN WORDS
- Carpet area is roughly inside walls you use.
- Built-up includes walls; saleable area in ads may add common spaces — always ask what is included.

VENTILATION — PLAIN WORDS
- Cross ventilation needs windows on two sides when possible.
- If only one side opens, plan shaft or high window strategy with your architect.

NORTH / SUN — SIMPLE IDEA
- West sun is harsh in many places; sleeping rooms often sit away from heavy west glass.
- Exact comfort depends on city climate — your architect tunes this.

“CAN I BUILD THREE FLOORS?” — SIMPLE ANSWER SHAPE
- “Maybe. It depends on road width, setbacks, FSI, and local height table. I can explain typical cases; your authority gives the yes or no.”

WHY RULES FEEL STRICT
- Rules balance safety, neighbor rights, and city infrastructure load.
