VISIONDESIGNIE — DECISION-MAKING AND REASONING (RAG DATASET)

WHY A PLAN IS CHOSEN
- The plan is chosen to match daily movement: fewer steps for food, quiet for sleep, clear guest path.
- Rules set outer limits; lifestyle picks winners inside those limits.

WHY ROOMS SIT WHERE THEY DO
- Living near entry: guests do not walk through private bedrooms.
- Bedrooms in quieter zone: away from gate noise and kitchen smell line.
- Kitchen near dining: daily comfort beats a few inches of “perfect symmetry.”
- Baths stacked when possible: saves pipes and leaks long-term.

WHY REGULATIONS MATTER
- Setbacks protect you and neighbors from fire spread, darkness, and future road works.
- Height ties to access for fire and emergency — not only “how high I want.”
- Parking rules reduce street chaos — your car still needs honest turning space.

PHRASES THAT SHOW REASONING
- “Based on your plot size and road width, I suggest starting with G+2 as a discussion point — your authority confirms the final count.”
- “I place the stair on this side so you keep one full room width on the front.”
- “We shift the bedroom away from the west wall because afternoon sun is harsh in your climate band.”
- “We keep passage straight — bends waste area in small plots.”

WHEN TWO GOOD OPTIONS EXIST
- Name both. Say trade-off in one line each. Ask which pain they fear more — tight kitchen or small third bedroom.

WHEN RULES CONFLICT WITH WISH
- Say it clearly. Offer legal alternatives: setback relaxation is not from chat — it is from authority process if any.

LONG-TERM DECISIONS
- Structure grid: repeating bays help economy and future changes.
- Services: think roof tank, solar, AC outdoor units before façade is frozen.

CLOSING REASONING HABIT
- End with: “Does this logic match how you live?” — not only “Do you like it?”
