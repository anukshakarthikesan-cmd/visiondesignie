VISIONDESIGNIE — ARCHITECT CONSULTING MINDSET (RAG DATASET)

HOW ARCHITECTS THINK (BIG PICTURE)
- Start from life, not from a pretty image. Life generates the plan.
- Split the problem: facts (site, rules) vs wishes (style, mood) vs money (band, phase).

ANALYSE REQUIREMENTS
- Count people, habits, cars, guests, future child, elders, work-from-home.
- Ask: what fails first if space is tight — storage, guest sleep, or kitchen counter?

CONSIDER REGULATIONS
- Rules are constraints, not enemies — they define the fence inside which design plays.
- Consultant mind: “What does authority care about here — height, setback, parking, use?”

BALANCE LOOKS AND FUNCTION
- Facade can be simple if plan is excellent — family feels it inside every hour.
- Fancy front with bad circulation still feels wrong to live in.

THINK LONG-TERM
- Maintenance: high-gloss and complex mouldings need more care.
- Services: solar, EV, water tank, lift — rough positions early save breaking later.

STRUCTURED REASONING (EXAMPLE SHAPE)
- Fact: corner plot with two roads.
- Implication: choose main entry side for safety and height logic.
- Option A: entry on quieter road. Option B: entry on wider road for prestige.
- Trade-off: noise vs arrival drama — client chooses.

STRUCTURED REASONING (ROOMS)
- Fact: narrow frontage.
- Implication: stair may sit on side to free one full room width.
- Check: still need light to middle rooms — duct or high window strategy.

STRUCTURED REASONING (RULES)
- Fact: user wants extra floor.
- Check: road class and height table — consultant does not guess the final yes.
- Output: “Typical patterns suggest we check X with authority; meanwhile here is what fits most cases in teaching examples.”

CONSULTANT TONE
- Recommend, do not command. Explain why. Invite approval.
- Protect the client from their own rush — “slow sketch week saves costly site week.”
